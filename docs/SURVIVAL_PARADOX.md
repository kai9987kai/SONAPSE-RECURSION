# Survival paradox

Each seed generates 48 shared threat and food schedules of at most 160 ticks each. A toy forager moves one position toward a food site when attentive, gains health when it reaches food, and loses two health units upon missing a threat. Zero health ends the episode. Four ticks of internal work arrive every seven ticks. Agents differ only in their workload and scheduling policies.

Outcomes include mean duration, completed work and interruptions. Native attention and emergency pre-emption have access to immediate hazard detection in this model. The full-horizon ceiling prevents measuring additional benefit above 160 ticks. Work currently has no intrinsic survival benefit. The comparison measures the imposed attention cost, and cannot establish an evolutionary disadvantage of imagination in real organisms.

## River crossing study

Eight additional agent policies face changing flow, construction materials and abstract hazards. A lumped pressure model projects the next twelve crossing ticks. Crossing safely yields food, waiting costs energy and planning occupies local attention. The external arm spends one local communication tick and separately charges offloaded work. The evolved arm inherits a warning threshold selected on distinct training environments. Twenty-four matched held-out episodes per regime per seed test quiet and hazardous conditions. The planner is conventional, and the model of water pressure is an engineering assumption. The benefit of a good crossing can offset the cost of deliberation.
